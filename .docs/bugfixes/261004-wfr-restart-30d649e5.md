  - Cause: the test's WaitForRunning polls every 100ms on its own goroutine,
    so `w.manager.stop()` lands at an arbitrary point in its polls. A poll sent
    before the client's mangos socket has noticed the manager close its
    connection, or one in flight as it stops, goes out on the dropped
    connection and waits the reply deadline (the larger of Timeout and a
    minute) before being resent. `client/doc.go` documents that an attempt in
    progress is not interrupted and can take up to that deadline, so the
    product behaves as documented; the test assumed every poll after `stop()`
    finds no connection. `stopSeenBy` alone is not enough: its ping takes the
    client's lock, so it waits behind a stuck poll, and the poll's ride-out
    then counts from when it was sent.
  - Fix (test only): `client/client_test.go`: `startRestartedWait` wraps the
    Scheduler's `*jobqueue.Client` in `pollGatedJobqueue`, whose
    `GetByEssenceContext` (the request WaitForRunning polls with) holds a read
    lock for the poll. `restartedWait.stopManager` takes the write lock, so it
    waits for any poll in progress (answered at once, as the manager is up)
    and holds the next one off, then calls `stopSeenBy` and releases the lock
    once the client has seen its connection drop. Every stop in
    `TestSchedulerWaitForRunningAcrossManagerRestart` uses it, so each poll
    after a stop is made while the manager is down from the client's side
    too. The subtests' assertions are unchanged: the wait still rides out each
    outage, gives up with `mangos.ErrSendTimeout` about RetryTime after the
    stop, and returns `context.Canceled` promptly when cancelled while the
    manager is down.
  - Mutant: `stopManager` calling `stopSeenBy` without taking the lock (in the
